Weighted grade calculator

Work out your course grade from syllabus category weights or module credits.

To find a weighted course grade, multiply each category’s percentage by its weight, add those products, then divide by the sum of the weights. For example, homework 92% at 20%, quizzes 84% at 20%, midterm 79% at 30% and final 88% at 30% gives (92×20 + 84×20 + 79×30 + 88×30) ÷ 100 = 85.3%.

Your course categories

Enter a score and a weight for each row you want counted. Weight is a syllabus percent or a credit value. Blank weights are not treated as equal.

How to use the weighted grade calculator

  1. List each category (or module) from your syllabus.
  2. Enter your average score for that category as a percent.
  3. Enter the category’s weight (percent of the course, or credit value).
  4. Read the weighted grade. Check the contribution column to see which rows move the average most.
  5. If some weight is still blank, treat the result as your grade so far, then use the final grade calculator for the remaining share.

The formula

Weighted grade = Σ (category % × weight) ÷ Σ (weights).

Weights can be syllabus percentages (20, 30, and so on) or university credits (10, 20, and so on). Only the ratios matter. The calculator divides by the total of the weights you entered.

Contribution of one category = category % × weight ÷ Σ (weights). When every weight adds to 100, that is the same as category % × (weight ÷ 100).

Full-syllabus worked example

CategoryScoreWeight
Homework92%20
Quizzes84%20
Midterm79%30
Final88%30
  1. Products: 92×20 = 1840, 84×20 = 1680, 79×30 = 2370, 88×30 = 2640.
  2. Sum of products: 1840 + 1680 + 2370 + 2640 = 8530.
  3. Sum of weights: 100.
  4. Weighted grade: 8530 ÷ 100 = 85.3%.

Your weighted grade is 85.3%.

Mid-semester when weights do not total 100

Homework 92% at weight 15, quizzes 84% at 20, labs 88% at 15 and a midterm 79% at 20. The final, worth 30, is still blank.

  • Graded weight so far: 15 + 20 + 15 + 20 = 70.
  • Products sum: 92×15 + 84×20 + 88×15 + 79×20 = 5960.
  • Grade so far: 5960 ÷ 70 ≈ 85.14%.

This is the average over the work entered so far, not a locked final course grade. For the score you need on the remaining weight, use the final grade calculator.

Using credits instead of percentages

ModuleMarkCredits
Core68%20
Elective A74%10
Elective B62%10

Weighted average = (68×20 + 74×10 + 62×10) ÷ 40 = 68.0%. The formula is the same. Credits are the weights.

Weighted vs simple average

A simple average treats every category the same. For the four scores in the full-syllabus example, (92 + 84 + 79 + 88) ÷ 4 = 85.75%. The weighted grade is 85.3%, because the lower midterm carries as much weight as the higher final. The weighted result can move either way: a strong score in a heavy category pulls it up, and a weak score in a heavy category pulls it down.

Questions and answers

How do I calculate a weighted grade?
Multiply each category percent by its weight, add the products, then divide by the total weight. Homework 92% at weight 20, quizzes 84% at 20, a midterm 79% at 30 and a final 88% at 30 gives (92×20 + 84×20 + 79×30 + 88×30) ÷ 100 = 85.3%.
What if my category weights do not add up to 100%?
That is normal mid-semester, when a final or another category is still blank. The calculator averages over the weights you entered. A locked final course grade usually needs every syllabus category, so the weights reach 100%.
Can I use credits instead of percentages?
Yes. Enter the module mark as the score and the credit value as the weight. The same formula applies to UK modules and to US credit hours.
How is this different from the final grade calculator?
This tool builds the course average from many weighted rows. The final grade calculator answers what you need on one remaining assessment when you already have a current grade and a target.
Should I enter every assignment or just category averages?
Prefer the category averages from your gradebook (homework average, quiz average) so they match the syllabus weights. Use one row per assignment only when each assignment is its own weighted item.
Why is my weighted grade different from a simple average?
Stronger scores in heavier categories pull the result up, and weaker scores in heavy categories pull it down. The contribution column shows which rows move the average most. In the worked example the weighted grade is 85.3% and the simple average of the same four scores is 85.75%.
Is anything saved or uploaded?
No. Calculations run in your browser only. Nothing is stored or uploaded.
