How long to read 1,000 words

By Ali Hamza

Last updated 6 November 2026

At the default speeds in StudyKit Hub’s reading time calculator, 1,000 words take 4 minutes 13 seconds to read silently and 7 minutes 9 seconds to speak aloud. Those two numbers come from the tool’s own formula, not from a guess.

A single answer to “how long to read 1,000 words” only exists once you choose a speed. This page uses the speeds the calculator starts with, shows the arithmetic, and says when to throw the defaults away.

The speeds the calculator starts with

Open the reading time calculator and you will see two boxes already filled in. Silent reading is 238 words per minute. Speaking is 140 words per minute. The page beside those boxes describes 238 as a typical adult silent reading speed for non-fiction, and it says presentations are often between 120 and 160 words per minute. The speaking default, 140, sits inside that range. You can replace either number with any speed from 50 to 1,000 words per minute. If you clear the boxes, the tool asks you to enter a speed. The reset button puts 238 and 140 back.

The tool does not look up a different speed for students, for a second language, or for a novel. If your cohort reads more slowly than an adult non-fiction average, type the speed you measured. The calculator will not invent one for you, and this guide will not invent one either.

How 1,000 words become 4 min 13 sec

Time in seconds is the word count divided by the speed in words per minute, multiplied by 60, then rounded up to the next whole second. For silent reading:

1,000 ÷ 238 × 60 = 252.1008… seconds, which rounds up to 253 seconds. 253 seconds is 4 minutes and 13 seconds.

For speaking:

1,000 ÷ 140 × 60 = 428.571… seconds, which rounds up to 429 seconds. 429 seconds is 7 minutes and 9 seconds.

A rough “about four minutes” or “about seven minutes” is fine in conversation. If you are booking a presentation slot or setting a class task, use the rounded-up figure the tool shows, because that is what you will see when you paste the same text. Zero words is reported as 0 sec. The rounding only bites once there is at least one word.

The same defaults at other lengths

You can scale the method, but do not scale the already-rounded 1,000-word time and expect the tool to match. Calculate each length on its own, then round up. At 238 and 140 words per minute the calculator produces these times:

If your text is 1,000 words exactly, you do not need the table. Paste it and read the result. The table is only a map for planning before the text is finished.

When the defaults will not match your task

Silent reading at 238 words per minute assumes you are moving through non-fiction without stopping to solve a problem. A methods chapter, a statute, a proof, or a page of quotations will take longer. Reading in a language you are still learning will take longer. Stopping to highlight or to write a margin note will take longer. The calculator does not add those pauses, because it cannot see them. It only counts words in the box.

Speaking at 140 words per minute is continuous speech. A seminar presentation with slides usually includes a pause while people look at a figure, and a question at the end. Add that time yourself. If you have rehearsed and timed a run-through, put that words-per-minute figure into the speaking box and ignore 140. The guide to reading time for speeches goes further on choosing a pace you can keep for a whole talk.

Listening is a separate job. The text to speech reader uses your browser’s voices and a speed you set on that page. Do not quote the table as the time that reader will take. Use the reader to hear a draft, and the calculator when you want a time from a word count and a speed.

Find your own speed in one minute

The calculator’s own instructions say to read for exactly one minute, paste what you read, and use that word count as your speed. Do it with material like the material you care about, then replace 238. For speaking, read aloud for one minute at the pace you would use in the room, and put that word count in the speaking box. Speeds outside 50 to 1,000 are rejected. A gap of ten words changes the silent time for a 1,000-word text by only a few seconds at 238 words per minute. Diagrams and note-taking change it much more.

Using the figure in a study plan

If a class sets about 1,000 words of reading, 4 minutes 13 seconds is only the silent time at 238 words per minute. Block longer if you must annotate. Reading your own draft aloud is the speaking figure, 7 minutes 9 seconds at 140, before pauses. Neither figure is a writing time. See the essay timer guide if the task is to produce the words. Paste the passage when you have it: the tool’s copied summary states the word count, both times and the speeds you used. The text stays in the browser tab.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to read 1,000 words?

At the reading time calculator’s default of 238 words per minute, 1,000 words take 4 minutes 13 seconds. The tool rounds up to the next whole second. Your own speed may be faster or slower, so replace 238 with a speed you have timed.

How long does it take to speak 1,000 words?

At the calculator’s default speaking speed of 140 words per minute, 1,000 words take 7 minutes 9 seconds. The tool’s own hint says presentations are often between 120 and 160 words per minute. Add time if you will pause for slides or questions.

Does the estimate include pictures, tables or thinking time?

No. The calculator times the words you paste and nothing else. Diagrams, equations, tables and the time you spend taking notes are extra. A 1,000-word chapter with figures will take longer than the figure in this guide.
