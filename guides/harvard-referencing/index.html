Harvard referencing for students

By Ali Hamza · Updated November 2026

“Harvard” usually means an author–date referencing family used widely in the UK, Australia and many European programmes taught in English. Unlike MLA’s page-focused literary style, Harvard keeps the year visible in the citation.

Reference examples

These are format examples, not real publications. Universities publish their own Harvard variants, so match the punctuation in your handbook.

Book chapter in an edited book

In-text: (Okeke, 2022)

Okeke, C. (2022) ‘Reading for seminars’, in J. Patel (ed.) University study skills. London: Northbridge Press, pp. 88–104.

Journal article with a DOI

In-text: (Adeyemi and Costa, 2021)

Adeyemi, L. and Costa, M. (2021) ‘Note-taking and recall in first-year seminars’, Journal of Learning Support, 14(2), pp. 45–61. doi: 10.1000/182.

10.1000/182 is the DOI Foundation’s sample identifier. It shows where a DOI sits. It is not a real article.

Website with no named author

In-text: (How to plan a revision week, 2024)

How to plan a revision week (2024) Study hours. Available at: https://example.org/revision-week (Accessed: 11 November 2026).

If an organisation is named and no person is, many guides use the organisation as the author instead of the page title. Some leave out the access date. Use the variant your department specifies.

Worked examples in Cite Them Right style

The examples above follow a common university variant, including a doi: prefix and a website name. The free generator follows Cite Them Right. These samples are not real publications. 10.1000/182 is the DOI Foundation’s sample identifier. It shows where a DOI sits.

Book

In-text: (Khan, 2024) or, with a page, (Khan, 2024, p. 12)

Khan, A. (2024) Study skills for university. 2nd edn. London: Pearson.

Leave the place of publication out when your handbook follows the current Cite Them Right edition: Khan, A. (2024) Study skills for university. 2nd edn. Pearson.

Journal article with a DOI

In-text: (Adeyemi and Costa, 2021)

Adeyemi, L. and Costa, M. (2021) ‘Note-taking and recall in first-year seminars’, Journal of Learning Support, 14(2), pp. 45–61. Available at: https://doi.org/10.1000/182.

A DOI does not need an accessed date. If there is no DOI and the article has a public URL, add Available at: URL (Accessed: day month year).

Web page with no date

In-text: (Khan, no date)

Khan, A. (no date) How to plan a revision week. Available at: https://example.org/revision-week (Accessed: 20 November 2026).

The accessed date is the day you read the page. The generator starts it at today, and you can change it.

Three authors, and four or more

Three authors, in-text: (Khan, Smith and Lee, 2024)

Khan, A., Smith, B. and Lee, C. (2024) Study skills for university. Pearson.

Four or more authors, in-text: (Khan et al., 2024)

Khan, A., Smith, B., Lee, C. and Patel, D. (2024) Study skills for university. Pearson.

Cite Them Right uses et al. in the text from four authors, and this generator still names every author in the reference list. Some courses want “Khan, A. et al.” in the reference list as well. Use the rule in your handbook, and use it the same way every time.

Secondary referencing (cited in)

Secondary referencing is for a source you have not read, when you know it only because another source quotes or cites it. Read the original where you can. If you cannot, name both works in the text and put only the source you read in the reference list. Use “cited in” when the later author summarises, and “quoted in” when the later author quotes: (Khan, 2018, cited in Smith, 2024, p. 12). Harvard does not use footnotes for this. The author and the year stay in the text.

Frequently asked questions

How do I cite a website in Harvard?

Name the author and the year in the text, then give the author, year, page title, site name, URL and access date in the reference list. If no person is named, use the organisation as the author, or the page title if no organisation is named either. Punctuation and whether you need an access date vary by university, so follow your handbook.

Does Harvard use footnotes?

Most Harvard variants do not use footnotes for sources. They use an author–date citation in the text and a reference list at the end. Some universities allow a footnote for a short comment, but the source still belongs in the reference list. Harvard is not one single manual, so check your department's guide.

How do I cite 3 authors in Harvard?

Cite Them Right names all three authors every time, for example (Khan, Smith and Lee, 2024). For four or more, the in-text citation uses the first author and et al., for example (Khan et al., 2024). The reference list names every author. Some handbooks use et al. in the reference list from four authors, and a few use et al. in the text from three. Follow your module handbook.

What is secondary referencing (cited in) in Harvard?

Secondary referencing is when you mention a source you have not read, because another source quotes or cites it. Read the original where you can. If you cannot, name both works in the text. Use cited in when the later author summarises, for example (Khan, 2018, cited in Smith, 2024, p. 12). Use quoted in when the later author quotes, for example (Khan, 2018, quoted in Smith, 2024, p. 12). List only the source you read, Smith (2024), in the reference list.
