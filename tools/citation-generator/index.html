Citation generator for Harvard, APA 7 and MLA 9

A Harvard reference lists the author's surname and initials, the year in round brackets, then the title and publication details, for example: Khan, A. (2024) Study skills for university. 2nd edn. London: Pearson. In the text you cite the author and year, such as (Khan, 2024) or (Khan, 2024, p. 12).

Citation details

Write each author as Last name, First name, for example: Khan, Ayesha. For an organisation, use braces: {World Health Organization}. Leave empty if there is no author. In the text, name one, two or three authors. For four or more, use the first author and et al. The reference list on this page names every author. Some handbooks use et al. in the reference list from four authors.
Type the title as you want it to appear. Harvard and APA use sentence case for the work title (only the first word and names capitalised). MLA uses title case. Journal names keep their own capitalisation.

How to use the citation generator

  1. Choose Harvard, APA or MLA, and the type of source.
  2. Fill in the details you have. Fields that do not apply to that source type are hidden.
  3. Check the citation as it updates, then press Copy citation. Italics are kept when you paste into a word processor that supports formatted text.

The patterns used

Harvard book: Surname, I. (Year) Title of the book. 2nd edn. Place: Publisher. Example: Khan, A. (2024) Study skills for university. 2nd edn. London: Pearson.

Harvard web page: Surname, I. (Year) Title of the page. Available at: URL (Accessed: 15 November 2026). No date is written as (no date). Example: Khan, A. (no date) How to plan a revision week. Available at: https://example.org/revision-week (Accessed: 15 November 2026).

Harvard journal article: Surname, I. (Year) ‘Title of article’, Journal Name, Volume(Issue), pp. 45–67. Available at: https://doi.org/… Example: Adeyemi, L. and Costa, M. (2021) ‘Note-taking and recall in first-year seminars’, Journal of Learning Support, 14(2), pp. 45–61. Available at: https://doi.org/10.1000/182.

Place of publication is optional. Leave it empty if your handbook follows the current Cite Them Right edition, which drops it. A DOI is given as Available at, with no accessed date. A journal or book URL without a DOI includes the accessed date. 10.1000/182 is the DOI Foundation’s sample identifier, not a real article.

APA 7 book: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of the book (2nd ed.). Publisher.

APA 7 journal article: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of the article. Journal Name, volume(issue), pages. https://doi.org/…

MLA 9 book: Last, First. Title of the Book. 2nd ed., Publisher, Year.

MLA 9 web page: Last, First. “Title of the Page.” Website Name, Day Month Year, URL.

In MLA, three or more authors become “First author, et al.” In APA, up to 20 authors are listed.

Questions and answers

Does the generator look up books or DOIs?
No. It works entirely in your browser and does not connect to any database, so you type in the details yourself. Nothing you type is uploaded.
What does n.d. mean?
APA uses “n.d.” (no date) when a source has no publication date. Leave the year empty to get it.
Why is the title not capitalised for me?
Automatic capitalisation often gets names, acronyms and other languages wrong. Type the title in the case your style needs. The case converter can help.
What is Harvard referencing?
Harvard referencing is an author-date system. In the text you give the author's surname and the year, such as (Khan, 2024). The reference list at the end is in alphabetical order by surname and gives the full details of each source you cited.
How do I reference three or more authors in Harvard?
In Cite Them Right Harvard, name one, two or three authors every time, for example (Khan and Smith, 2024) or (Khan, Smith and Lee, 2024). For four or more authors, the in-text citation uses the first author and et al., for example (Khan et al., 2024). This generator names every author in the reference list. Some university guides use et al. in the reference list from four authors, and a few use et al. in the text from three authors. Follow your module handbook.
What is the difference between Harvard and APA 7?
Both are author-date styles, but the punctuation differs. APA puts a full stop after the year bracket, as in (2024). Harvard does not. APA joins two authors with & and Harvard uses and. APA writes 2nd ed. and Harvard writes 2nd edn. Harvard web pages use Available at and an accessed date. APA uses a DOI link or URL, and an accessed date only in some website references. Harvard also has many university versions, so follow your handbook if it differs.
