UK degree classifications explained

By Ali Hamza · Updated November 2026

UK undergraduate honours degrees are usually awarded as First (70%+), Upper Second / 2:1 (60–69%), Lower Second / 2:2 (50–59%) or Third (40–49%) from a credit- and year-weighted average. Year weights and borderline rules differ by university — your programme handbook wins.

Try your own marks in the UK degree classification calculator. It applies the usual bands to the credits and year weights you type. It does not apply one university’s borderline rule for you.

One handbook reminder. Exact boundaries, discounted credits, resit caps and uplift rules are local. Use this page for the usual pattern, then confirm against your classification regulations.

The usual bands

Common undergraduate exit awards are First-class honours, Upper Second (2:1), Lower Second (2:2) and Third. Some regulations also allow an ordinary or pass degree without honours. Integrated master’s degrees and Scottish pathways use related rules that are not the same as a three-year bachelor’s in England.

ClassUsual weighted average
First Class Honours (1st)70% and above
Upper Second (2:1)60–69%
Lower Second (2:2)50–59%
Third Class (3rd)40–49%
Below thirdUnder 40% (ordinary or fail, under local rules)

Universities UK’s 2021 principles for degree-algorithm design say that, typically although not always, the boundaries are a first at 70 or above, an upper second at 60–69, a lower second at 50–59 and a third at 40–49. Some awarding bodies mark on another scale, for example 1 to 20, and then align the result with those classes. The UK Standing Committee for Quality Assessment describes what the four classes should represent as learning. Those descriptions do not fix one percentage formula, and in Scotland, Northern Ireland and Wales they are advice. Each university still sets its own algorithm.

University types and variations

A three-year bachelor’s in England, Wales or Northern Ireland often counts later years more heavily than the first, but the weights are local. University College London’s Academic Manual for 2026–27 prints three honours schemes. Scheme A counts the best 90 credits of year 1 at weight 1, the best 90 of year 2 at weight 3, and all 120 credits of year 3 at weight 5. Scheme C gives year 1 a weight of 0. Another university need not use any of these.

An integrated master’s adds a year. At UCL that fourth year is weighted 5, the same as the third. Some awards have no class: UCL’s MBBS is one, because a professional body requires that. A taught master’s there is Distinction, Merit or Pass, starting at 69.50 for a distinction, not a first or a 2:1.

Scottish honours degrees are often four years, and the early years may not enter the class. Edinburgh classifies most undergraduate degrees on the third and fourth years, so a broad first and second year does not lower the class. Study abroad can put the class on the final year only, and its integrated masters use the final three years. Its marking scheme prints the class as 70–100, 60–69, 50–59 and 40–49.

How the average is built

Where a university uses percentages, the usual sum has two steps, and it is the sum this site’s calculator uses. First combine the counting modules in a year: add mark times credits, then divide by the credits that count. Then combine the year averages: add year average times year weight, then divide by the sum of the weights. A larger module moves the year. A heavier year moves the degree.

Worked example

These year weights are 0, 40 and 60. Year 1 is passed and then dropped. They are an illustration, not UCL’s 1, 3 and 5 and not a national rule.

Year 2 is 20 credits at 65% and 40 credits at 62%. The year average is (65 × 20 + 62 × 40) ÷ 60 = 63%. Year 3 is 40 credits at 72% and 80 credits at 69%. The year average is (72 × 40 + 69 × 80) ÷ 120 = 70%. The degree average is (63 × 40 + 70 × 60) ÷ 100 = 67.2%, an upper second on the usual bands.

If the 80-credit mark rises from 69% to 75%, the degree average becomes 69.6%. A strict cut-off at 70 still calls that a 2:1. UCL’s rule calls 69.50 or above a first, so the same figure would be a first there. These weights are still not UCL’s: the class depends on the rule as well as the average. The calculator will show 67.2 or 69.6 if you enter the marks with weights 0, 40 and 60. It will not apply the board’s next step.

Borderline rules

UCL writes its borderline as a test. 69.50% or above is a first. From 68.50% up to, but not including, 69.50%, it is still a first when at least half the final-year credits are at 70. The same shape makes 59.50 a 2:1, and 58.50 a 2:1 when half the final-year credits are at 60. A third needs 40.00, with no zone under that line. The mark is rounded to two decimal places first. Other boards leave a point or two under 60 or 70 to an exam board. A calculator cannot apply that step, and a capped resit at UCL enters the class as the capped mark.

What this number is not

A UK class is a weighted percentage band, not a US 4.0 GPA. Report the award on the transcript, and convert only when the organisation that asked publishes a method. For the credit-hour sum used on many US transcripts, see how to calculate GPA. For Dutch 1–10 marks, see the Dutch grading scale. A single module exam is a different question: use the final grade calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage is a 2:1?

On the usual bands, an upper second is 60 to 69 and a first is 70 or above. Universities UK calls those boundaries typical, not universal. UCL treats 59.50 as a 2:1 and 69.50 as a first, after rounding to two decimal places. Your handbook can differ.

Does every university weight the years the same way?

No. UCL's usual scheme weights years 1, 3 and 5, and another of its schemes gives the first year a weight of 0. Edinburgh usually classifies on the third and fourth years only. Integrated master's years and study abroad are weighted locally too.

What is a borderline?

It is a published rule for an average just short of the next class. At UCL, 68.50 with at least half of the final-year credits at 70 is a first, and the test is automatic. Other universities leave a point or two to an exam board. A calculator cannot apply a rule you have not entered.

Is a UK first the same as a 4.0 GPA?

No. A UK class is a percentage band. Report the class on the transcript unless the organisation that asked publishes a conversion. A taught master's at UCL is Distinction, Merit or Pass, not a first or a 2:1.
