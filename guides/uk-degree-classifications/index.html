UK degree classifications explained

Study guide · Updated 1 November 2026

UK undergraduate honours degrees are usually awarded as First (70%+), Upper Second / 2:1 (60–69%), Lower Second / 2:2 (50–59%) or Third (40–49%) from a credit- and year-weighted average. Year weights and borderline rules differ by university — your programme handbook wins.

One handbook reminder. Exact boundaries, discounted credits, resit caps and uplift rules are local. Use this page for the usual pattern, then confirm against your classification regulations.

The main classes

Common undergraduate exit awards are First-class honours, Upper second (2:1), Lower second (2:2), Third-class and, in some regulations, an ordinary or pass degree without honours. Integrated master’s and Scottish pathways can use related but not identical rules.

ClassUsual weighted average
First Class Honours (1st)70% and above
Upper Second (2:1)60–69%
Lower Second (2:2)50–59%
Third Class (3rd)40–49%
Below thirdUnder 40% (ordinary / fail — local rules)

How averages are built

Modules are first combined into a year average by credits: Σ (mark × credits) ÷ Σ credits. Those year averages are then combined into a degree average using year weights: Σ (year average × year weight) ÷ Σ weights. First year often carries 0% weight (must be passed, but does not move the class); later years usually count more. Some algorithms discard a lowest mark band or treat study-abroad credit specially — do not invent your own formula.

Worked example (0 / 40 / 60)

Year 2 averages 63% at weight 40%; year 3 averages 70% at weight 60%. Degree average = (63 × 40 + 70 × 60) ÷ 100 = 67.2% → Upper Second (2:1) on the usual boundaries. Try the same numbers in the UK degree classification calculator.

Borders and discretion

Many universities publish borderline rules when an average sits within about 1–2 percentage points of the next class (for example near 60% or 70%). Those rules may look at the share of final-year credit in the higher band, rounding, or exam-board discretion. A public calculator can flag the zone; it cannot apply your university’s uplift. Improving a final-year mark usually moves the class more than polishing an early-year module already locked in.

Comparing systems

A UK class is a weighted percentage band, not a US 4.0 GPA. Convert only when a receiver publishes a method, and report the award as printed on your transcript. Dutch 1–10 marks also map awkwardly — treat crosswalk tables as guidance unless an official body certifies them. For US-style GPA maths see How to calculate GPA; for Dutch marks see the Dutch grading scale guide.
